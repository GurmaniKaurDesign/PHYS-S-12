Embrace

A self-sizing, breathable, smart neck brace that prioritises patient comfort and disaster relief without compromising immobilisation. A device that feels like an extension of the self — not an intrusion.

Comfort Compliance Care Soft Robotics Biomedical Design

The Problem

Standard emergency and post-surgical neck braces are built like ancient torture devices. They trap heat and sweat, compress the jaw, and cause severe pressure ulcers on the chin and collarbones. Paramedics have to guess sizing under high-stress conditions. Patients endure weeks of discomfort in a device not designed for their body.

Comfort is not a luxury feature in medical equipment. It's compliance. Patients who aren't in pain wear their braces correctly.

Traditional cervical collar — pressure mapping shows ulcer-prone contact zones

The Concept

Embrace takes an exoskeleton-like approach — a 3D-printed breathable lattice shell housing soft pneumatic McKibben actuators that automatically conform to the patient's neck when inflated. A handmade Velostat pressure sensor reads fit in real time, and an Arduino Uno R3 controls the inflation pump until the correct pressure is reached.

Self-Sizing

McKibben pneumatic actuators — soft robotic bladders inside a braided mesh — expand radially when inflated, distributing pressure evenly across the neck surface. No manual sizing. No guessing under pressure.

Breathable Shell

A 3D-printed PLA lattice structure allows airflow through the brace, eliminating heat and sweat buildup — the primary cause of skin breakdown in long-term brace wear.

Smart Feedback

Pressure sensing via FSR detects under-tightening and over-tightening. A haptic or LED output tells the patient and caregiver the brace is correctly positioned — without needing to remove it to check.

Objective

Study current brace pressure points and FDA regulations, then design a replacement that integrates wearable technology and soft robotics to create a device that is not only functional but humane.

System Architecture

Input

Pressure Sensor

FSR reads contact force between brace and neck surface continuously

Controller

Arduino Uno R3

Maps pressure to fit zones — too loose, good fit, too tight

Output A

Air Pump

Inflates McKibben actuators until target pressure is reached

Output B

Solenoid Valve

Opens to vent air automatically when pressure exceeds safe threshold

Output C

Haptic / LED

Confirms correct fit to patient and caregiver without removing brace

Structure

Lattice Shell

3D printed PLA exoskeleton — breathable, biocompatible contact surfaces

Proof of Concept

Week 4's programming assignment became the first live test of Embrace's core sensing loop. With no commercial FSR or McKibben actuator available in the lab, both were built from scratch — Velostat + copper tape for the sensor, latex tube + braided mesh for the actuator.
