Printed in Crimson Red

A 3D printed tie for my final project presentation, printed in Harvard's Crimson Red to match my red suit. Mid-print fabric embedding turns a simple extrusion into a composite material: rigid PLA structure with soft mesh woven into its layers, held to the print bed with magnets during the pause.

This week explored two techniques: mid-print material embedding and 3D scanning. The tie started as a SolidWorks model, was sliced for the Prusa MK4S, then paused after the first two layers to insert a net fabric mesh before continuing. The result is a wearable object that is part print, part textile - so I can wear my project to my final presentation and do that in style!

Process

01

Modelling in SolidWorks

Designed the tie form in SolidWorks — a classic necktie silhouette with a 0.1" offset and 0.5" extrusion to embed fabric and still hold its shape after printing.

02

Slicing for Mid-Print Pause

Imported the STL into PrusaSlicer and added a filament change/pause command at layer 2. This gives just enough base to hold the fabric flat before the upper layers lock it in.

03

Embedding the Fabric

At the pause, the printer moved to the side and waited. The net fabric mesh was laid flat on the print surface, and secured with magnets on the print bed to prevent it from shifting when printing resumed. The printer then continued, encapsulating the mesh between layers.

Placing and securing the mesh fabric mid-print

04

Final Print

The printer resumed and completed the tie with the fabric fully embedded between the PLA layers creating a composite material that is rigid in structure but has a soft textile quality at the surface in contact with skin.

Completed tie — fresh off the Prusa MK4S

What worked

The magnets were key — without them the mesh shifted when the printhead moved, causing misalignment. Securing the fabric flat before resuming gave clean encapsulation.

What I'd change

Pausing at layer 2 meant very little PLA below the fabric. Next time I'd pause at layer 4–5 for a stronger base before embedding, especially for larger fabric pieces. I also want to experiment with light and creating slots in my print for conceling the light source to create a glowing effect through the mesh.
